## 7. Claude's feedback on that plan

### What works
1. **A recurring Sam & Coco series.** Coco becomes a character people come back for.
2. **Coco wearing the day's colour** (bandana, bow or harness). Simple and instantly recognisable.
3. **Sam wearing the caps, products sitting in the scene.** Matches the TENDS rule that people notice the dog's behaviour before the product.
4. **Everyday small-dog stories** (e.g. coming home). Matches the TENDS **Recognition** belief: show an owner something they already know but have never seen said.

### Three things to change

**1. The sample colour order doesn't match the real calendar.**
The sample starts with Yellow and includes **Pink**, which isn't one of the days. Everything was remapped to the real dates. The **Fuchsia Cap** covers Red day (Day 3), the closest to the sample's "pink cap" idea.

**2. The sample copy is a different campaign from the brief.**
It's nine days of nutrition argument ("Dogs need meat", "Pure isn't a nutrition claim", "Formulation matters"). That clashes with:
- the brief itself: fun, quirky, takeaway on the colour's meaning
- TENDS rules: captions don't teach or sell, and Declaration posts should be rare (about 4 in 30)

Recommendation: tell a quirky story every day and save the big statement for Day 9.

**3. Sam should be in the scene but not the star.**
In Sammy & Coco the human performs. TENDS rules say the human appears only partly (hands, legs, the cap from behind) and never as the subject. **Coco carries each episode. Sam is the straight man.**

### Question posed
Does this mix (quirky stories + colour meaning + a nutrition statement only on Days 4 and 9) work, or should the nutrition argument stay the backbone as in the sample? **Not yet answered explicitly.** The user moved on to the sitcom format, which builds on the story-led approach.

## 8. The TENDS Instagram system rules that apply

Loaded from the `tends-instagram-strategist` skill. These are the rules the series is checked against.

### Core idea
- Goal: **perception → recognition → belief**. "Not what people follow. How people start to see."
- Small dogs aren't a size segment. They're a different world: higher energy per kg, tighter margins, constant closeness to people.
- Instagram **shows and repeats**. The website explains.

### Four beliefs (never stated, always present)
| Belief | Code | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Defiance | Df | Small dogs don't behave within the limits people assume |
| Necessity | Ns | Their constraints and needs are real, not approximate |
| Intention | In | Caring for a small dog is deliberate. Every decision matters more |
| Recognition | Rc | The relationship is unusually close. Nothing goes unseen for long |

### Four expressions
| Expression | Code | Captures |
|---|---|---|
| Small Moments | M | Quiet everyday closeness, noticing |
| Small Attitude | A | Confidence, composure bigger than their size |
| Small Rituals | R | Feeding, hydration, care, routine |
| Declaration | D | Direct language. Rare and controlled (about 4 in 30) |

### Formats
Still (S), Reel (Re), Carousel (C).

### Rules most relevant to this series
1. **If the human becomes the subject, it breaks the world.** → Coco is the lead. Sam is partly in frame.
2. **If the product is noticed before the behaviour, it breaks the world.** → Products appear inside the action (a treat given, water taken, balm applied).
3. **If it feels like content, it is wrong. If it feels like behaviour, it belongs.**
4. **No pet environments, no dog-park framing.** The dog belongs in the human world: home, doorways, streets.
5. **Captions** name what the viewer already knows. Short. No explaining, selling, tips or product names.
6. **Sign-off on every post:** ✦ Small breed only. Big care always.

### Where the sitcom pushes against these rules
- A sitcom is performed, while the brand rule says "no performance, no host energy". **Resolution:** Coco performs (dog behaviour is the comedy). Sam reacts as the straight man, mostly partial or from behind.
- A laugh track is a stylised device. It's accepted here as the series' signature, because the jokes still come from real, recognisable dog behaviour.

## 11. The sitcom series format

### Title sequence (about 3 seconds)
```
TENDS presents
COCO & SAM            ← alternatives: "Small Talk", "Nine Nights with Coco"
Episode 1 · The One Where Someone's at the Door
```
- Shown on a background in **that day's colour**
- Short theme-tune sting
- **"The One Where…"** episode titles are a nod to F.R.I.E.N.D.S
- ⚠️ **Design your own logo and music.** Don't copy the F.R.I.E.N.D.S dotted logo or theme song; both are trademarked/copyrighted.

### Episode structure (30–45 second Reel)
| Part | Length | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Cold open | ~5s | The setup (e.g. keys rattling in the door) |
| 2. Title card | ~3s | TENDS presents → COCO & SAM → Episode N · Title |
| 3. The scene | 20–30s | The story, built to one punchline on Coco |
| 4. The tag | ~5s | Coco does one last thing after the joke, then the day's colour-meaning line on screen |
| 5. Sign-off | — | ✦ Small breed only. Big care always. |

### Cinematography
- **One fixed apartment set every episode:** sofa, front door, kitchen counter, rug. Viewers learn the room, which is half of why sitcoms feel familiar.
- **Multi-camera sitcom look:** wide static shot of the room, cutting to medium shots and reaction close-ups.
- **Lighting:** bright, warm, even. No moody shadows.
- **Coco's close-ups are shot from her eye level.** Her deadpan reaction is the punchline.

### Audio
- **Laugh track** on Coco's reactions
- **"Awww"** on soft moments
- **Applause** when she enters

### Casting and roles
- **Coco:** the lead. Small brown poodle, ~5 kg, wears the day's colour (bandana, bow or harness).
- **Sam:** the straight man. Reacts and doesn't perform. Often legs, hands or cap only. Wears the TENDS cap on cap days (2, 3, 4, 6).
