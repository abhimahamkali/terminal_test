# 00 — Strategy & Positioning

## 1. The single most important decision: you are not selling rasam

Your intuition is correct, and it's the load-bearing insight of this whole business.

- **"Rasam"** anchors the product to a ₹40/kg home-cooked commodity that every target customer's mother makes better and free. It caps price at commodity level and pre-loads "why would I pay for this?"
- **"Instant"** signals compromise, MSG, Maggi-adjacent, cheap. It is a discount word.
- Both words also shrink the market to people who already know and like rasam — i.e. the smallest possible TAM, and the segment least willing to pay a premium.

**Reframe: you are launching India's first savoury hot drink — the third option next to chai and coffee.**

This does four things at once:
1. Creates a category where you are #1 instead of entering a category where you are a weak #4 (behind MTR, Sakthi, Aachi, home-made).
2. Unlocks a price ceiling. A "hot drink" is compared to coffee (₹150/cup) not to masala (₹0.40/g).
3. Nails both niches you named: **non-coffee drinkers** (no caffeine) and **plant-based cafés** (naturally vegan, no dairy — unlike chai).
4. Gives you an occasion nobody owns: the **4pm slump** and the **post-dinner wind-down** — the two times of day when chai and coffee both fail (crash, sleep disruption).

## 2. The marketing template to copy — LMNT, not MTR

You asked for the hook that protein and zero-sugar brands use. Here is the closest analogue and it is almost perfectly transferable:

**LMNT (US)** built a ~$200M brand selling *a sachet of salt*. Their entire proposition:
- Radical subtraction as the hero: "No sugar. No colours. No junk."
- A number-led stat block on the front of pack (sodium/potassium/magnesium in mg).
- A savoury, salty drink positioned as performance + hydration, not as food.
- Founder/athlete content, not brand advertising.

Your product is structurally the same thing with better flavour and a real culinary heritage. Your front-of-pack stat block:

> **0 g sugar · 0 mg caffeine · ~6 kcal · 0 dairy · 1 ingredient list you can read**

That is the hook. Not "instant rasam". Not even "gut health" as the headline — see the claims warning below.

## 3. Where "gut health" fits (and the legal trap)

Gut health is the right cultural current — but in India it is a **regulated health claim**, governed by the FSS (Advertising and Claims) Regulations, 2018. If you print "improves digestion", "debloats", "appetite booster", or "boosts gut health" on pack or in an ad, you must hold scientific substantiation, and you risk being pushed into the Health Supplements & Nutraceuticals regulations — which destroys your "easy to register, boring category" requirement. See `02-compliance-india.md`.

**The workaround that keeps you both cool and legal:**

| Don't say (claim) | Do say (culture / sensory / tradition) |
|---|---|
| "Debloats you" | "The drink your grandmother served *after* dinner, not before" |
| "Boosts digestion" | "Tamarind, black pepper, cumin, asafoetida" (let the ingredients imply it) |
| "Appetite booster" | "Savoury. Warm. Zero sugar." |
| "Gut-healthy" | "0 sugar, 0 caffeine, 0 dairy" (subtraction claims are factual and safe) |
| "Ayurvedic benefits" | "2,000 years old. Zero grams of sugar." |

Ingredient-led implication is legal. Physiological promises are not. The ingredient deck does the persuading; you never have to make the claim yourself. This is exactly how the zero-sugar category works — they sell on what is *absent*, which is always factually provable.

**Warning on influencers:** ASCI's influencer guidelines and FSSAI both treat a paid creator's words as *your* advertising. If a creator you pay says "this debloats me," that is your claim and your liability. Brief creators in writing, keep the brief on record, and cap them at sensory + subtraction language.

## 4. Segments, ranked by how hard they are to sell

| # | Segment | Why they buy | Difficulty | Revenue role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hotel in-room amenity tray** | Sits next to the tea/coffee sachets. Zero prep, zero wastage, zero training. Differentiator for a regional chain. | Low | Visibility + steady trickle |
| 2 | **Hotel banquets / conference tea breaks** | 300–800 sachets consumed in one afternoon. Replaces a labour-heavy welcome drink. | Medium | **Real volume** |
| 3 | **Plant-based / specialty cafés** | A menu item nobody else has, ₹0 prep, sold at ₹90–150. Insane café margin. | Medium | Volume + cultural credibility |
| 4 | **Gyms & studios** | Beetroot and lemon SKUs as *savoury electrolytes*. Salty warm drink post-workout. | Medium | Volume + the LMNT narrative |
| 5 | **Corporate pantries / co-working** | The caffeine-free option for the 4pm slump. Bulk, repeatable, fast POs. | Low-Medium | Underrated — add this |

Note #5 — corporate pantries. You didn't list it, but it is the easiest B2B sale in this whole deck: no chef politics, no purchase committee, no 90-day payment terms, and the 4pm-caffeine-free story lands instantly with a 200-person office. Add it to Phase 1.

## 5. The channel logic, stated honestly

Hotels are a **trial and credibility machine, not a revenue machine.** Run the math:

> A 100-room hotel at 65% occupancy = ~1,950 room-nights/month. At 1 sachet consumed per 3 room-nights = ~650 sachets = **₹4,875/month at ₹7.50/sachet.**

You would need ~40 such hotels to fill one production line. That is a two-year sales effort for ₹2L/month. **So do not mistake the hotel channel for the business.** Its job is:
- To put the product in a stranger's hand with a premium context halo (this is worth more than any ad).
- To generate the search behaviour that makes quick commerce work.
- To give you logo credibility ("served at Minerva, Avira") that opens cafés, gyms, corporates and eventually export.

Volume comes from banquets, cafés, gyms and corporates. Margin comes from quick commerce. Hotels buy you the right to be there. Your instinct on this was right — the numbers back it.

## 6. Naming

Rules: must not contain "rasam" or "instant"; must be pronounceable to a non-Indian buyer (you want export later); must be clearable in Trade Marks Class 30 (and ideally Class 32); must survive being said out loud in a Hyderabad café and a London supermarket.

Four directions:

**A. Abstract + hard-working descriptor (recommended — the LMNT route)**
Brand means nothing; the descriptor and stat block do all the work.
`SIPP` · `TARR` · `OKRA` · `NOON`
→ Lockup: **NOON — Savoury Sipping Broth · 0 sugar · 0 caffeine**

**B. Heritage word that isn't "rasam"**
`SAARU` (Kannada for rasam) · `CHAARU` (Telugu) · `RASA` (Sanskrit: essence/taste)
→ Fresher to a national audience, still authentic, but carries some of the same commodity gravity. "Rasa" is heavily used — expect trademark conflict.

**C. Category-creating name**
`THE THIRD CUP` · `SAVOURY HOUR` · `ANTI-COFFEE`
→ Strongest positioning, weakest as a long-term brand asset.

**D. Occasion name**
`4PM` · `AFTER` · `WINDDOWN`
→ `4PM` is genuinely good: it names the problem, it's caffeine-adjacent territory, it's short, it's Instagram-native as a hashtag.

**My pick: Direction A or `4PM`.** Run a Class 30 + Class 32 search before you fall in love with any of them (see compliance doc).

## 7. The GenZ virality mechanic

Don't try to make a *powder* viral. Make a **ritual** viral. The format is the content.

- **The savoury shot.** 3 g bag, 60 ml of hot water, served in a clear espresso glass. Dark red beetroot liquid, steam, a teabag string hanging over the rim. That is a native Instagram Reel frame, and it is visually unlike anything in Indian F&B.
- **Café menu item is the unlock.** "Savoury Shot — ₹90" on a Hyderabad or Bangalore specialty-café menu does more for you than ₹5L of influencer spend. It gives the product third-party validation and a photographable context you don't own.
- **The show-off behaviour** you're looking for is: *"I don't do coffee."* That is already an identity people perform online (sober-curious, caffeine-free, anti-cortisol, no-sugar). You are giving them a prop for an identity they already want to signal. That's the correct viral mechanic — not "try this weird rasam".
- **Rana Daggubati / similar:** the ask is not an endorsement post. It's a single organic-looking morning frame. Costly and unreliable. Lower down the priority list than the café menu play.
- **Content cadence:** one product-centric frame daily, but the content pillar should be *"the savoury drink"* as a concept (miso, broth, bouillon, rasam, sotanghon) — build the category, then own it. Educating people that savoury hot drinks exist is your actual marketing job for year one.

## 8. The honest risks

1. **No category exists.** Every attempt at a third Indian hot drink has struggled against chai and coffee. You are not fighting MTR; you are fighting habit. Budget 18–24 months to a repeatable channel, not 6.
2. **The tea-bag format may not deliver rasam.** This is a real product risk, not a marketing one. See `01-product-packaging.md` §2 — solve it before spending on branding.
3. **Capacity is your GTM bottleneck.** 6 kg/day = 2,000 sachets/day. One 500-pax conference is a quarter of a day's output. You need a co-packing relationship from day one or you will win a banquet order and fail to serve it.
4. **Hotel working capital.** Hotels pay in 60–90 days. Cafés and corporates pay in 15–30. Weight your early mix toward the fast payers or you will be profitable on paper and broke in the bank.
5. **Custom hotel branding MOQs.** Rotogravure printing MOQs will bury you. Solved in `01-product-packaging.md` §4.
