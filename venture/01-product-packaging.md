# 01 — Product, Format & Packaging

## 1. SKU architecture

Three flavours × two pack formats, one production line, one costing.

| Flavour | Positioning | Lead channel | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Original** | The classic. Pepper-cumin-tamarind. | Hotels, corporates | Your volume SKU. Must be the safest, most universally acceptable taste. |
| **Lemon** | Bright, light, hydration-coded | Gyms, cafés | Best "savoury electrolyte" story. Lowest risk flavour for a first-timer. |
| **Beetroot** | Earthy, deep red, pre-workout coded | Gyms, Instagram | **This is your hero for content.** The colour is the marketing asset. Nitrate/beet story is already understood by gym-goers. |

Strategic note: Beetroot is your *acquisition* SKU (visual, novel, shareable) and Original is your *retention* SKU. Don't over-index production on Original just because it's the "real" rasam.

## 2. The format problem — solve this first, before anything else

**Rasam powder in a flat filter-paper tea bag will probably disappoint.** This is the biggest technical risk in the business and it is cheap to resolve now and ruinous to discover after you've bought tooling.

Why it fails:
- Traditional rasam powder is built on **toor dal, coriander seed and tamarind pulp** — largely *insoluble*. In a filter bag, the dal and pulp stay in the bag. You get coloured, weakly-flavoured, thin water with no body. Rasam's character is partly its slight turbidity and mouthfeel.
- Flat filter paper has a pore size designed for tea leaf extraction, not for letting spice fines through. Your fines are exactly what you want in the cup.
- Tamarind is **hygroscopic**. It will cake and go sticky in the bag.

Three format options — prototype all three in week 1, they cost ~₹10,000 total:

**Option A — Reformulated infusion bag (pyramid mesh)**
- Reformulate to a high-solubility blend: spice **oleoresins/extracts** or very fine fines + **soluble tamarind extract powder** + salt + a soluble body agent, targeting ≥85% dissolution.
- Use a **pyramid nylon/PLA mesh** bag, not flat filter paper — larger pore, lets fines through, and looks dramatically more premium.
- Pros: the "dip and serve" theatre, no residue in the cup, no spoon, no training — this is what makes the hotel and café sale easy. Premium perception.
- Cons: higher bag cost (₹1.00–1.50 vs ₹0.40), reformulation R&D, and you will never get 100% of real rasam's body.

**Option B — Stick-pack / dissolve sachet**
- 3 g free powder, stir into hot water. Fully dissolves. Tastes closest to real rasam.
- Pros: cheapest packaging, best taste fidelity, simplest to manufacture.
- Cons: loses the theatre. Needs a spoon and a stir. Weaker premium cue. Harder hotel amenity sale (the tea-bag form factor is *why* it fits on the tray).

**Option C — Both** ← recommended
- **Infusion bag** → hotels, cafés, gifting, retail. The format *is* the product story.
- **Stick pack** → gyms, corporates, bulk, quick-commerce value packs.
- Same blend, same fill weight, two secondary formats. Marginal added complexity, meaningfully wider channel access.

**Action: before you spend a rupee on branding, make 20 cups.** Blind-test flat filter bag vs pyramid mesh vs stir-in against a home-made rasam, with 10 people. If the infusion bag scores below ~70% of home-made on flavour intensity, reformulate or go stick-pack-led. Everything downstream depends on this answer.

## 3. Moisture barrier — a non-negotiable cost you may not have budgeted

Tamarind + salt + spice fines at 3 g is extremely hygroscopic. In a paper envelope it will clump and lose aroma in weeks.

- You need a **metallised laminate (PET/MET-PET/PE) individual envelope**, not paper or glassine. ≈ ₹0.80–1.10 each, printed.
- Blend moisture must be held **below ~5%**; specify it in your co-packer agreement and test it per batch.
- Target shelf life: **12 months**. Validate with an accelerated shelf-life study at a NABL lab (budgeted in `03-financials.md`).

Do not let anyone sell you a kraft-paper "premium eco" envelope for this product. It will fail, and it will fail *after* the hotel has stocked 2,000 units.

## 4. Two packagings, identical cost — how to actually do it

Your constraint: hotel SKU with custom branding and retail SKU, **same costing**. This is achievable, but only if you avoid the MOQ trap.

**The trap:** custom rotogravure printing means a cylinder/plate set at ₹25,000–40,000 *per design*, with a film MOQ that translates to roughly 50,000+ envelopes per design. Three flavours × retail = 3 designs. Add one custom design per hotel and you are dead — you'd be sitting on 50,000 envelopes for a hotel that orders 650/month, i.e. 6 years of stock.

**The fix — a two-layer system:**

| Layer | Approach | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Base envelope** | ONE generic metallised envelope design, printed rotogravure at real volume. Carries your brand, the stat block, and all mandatory FSSAI text. Flavour indicated by a printed colour band OR by a die-cut window. | ₹0.85 (at volume) |
| **Retail identity** | The base envelope *is* the retail pack. Flavour differentiated by a small digitally-printed flavour label or a per-flavour colour variant of the same plate. | +₹0.00–0.15 |
| **Hotel identity** | Same base envelope + a **digitally printed adhesive label** carrying the hotel's logo, applied at packing. MOQ for digital labels: ~1,000 units, ~₹0.40–0.90 each depending on run. | +₹0.40–0.90 |

Then **price both SKUs off the same cost base** and absorb the hotel label cost into the hotel price (which is anyway your lower-margin channel, and the hotel is paying for branding — charge for it, or use it as the negotiation give). Costing stays uniform at the blend + bag + base-envelope level, which is what actually matters for your P&L and your quoting.

For hotels ordering >20,000/year, graduate them to their own printed film and the cost drops back.

**Alternative worth testing:** a plain premium envelope + the hotel's branding on the **outer display carton and the teabag tag** only. Many hotels care more about the carton on the amenity tray than the individual wrapper, and tags/cartons are cheap to customise digitally.

## 5. Spec sheet (target)

```
Net weight            3.0 g ± 0.15 g per sachet
Format                Pyramid infusion bag (primary) / stick pack (secondary)
Primary pack          Metallised laminate envelope, printed
Serve                 3 g in 150 ml water at 90-95 degC, steep 90 sec
Yield                 1 cup (150 ml)
Moisture              < 5.0%
Shelf life            12 months (to be validated)
Sugar                 0 g
Caffeine              0 mg
Energy                ~5-8 kcal per serve
Allergens             Declare: asafoetida (wheat-based compounding is common -> GLUTEN RISK)
Veg status            Green veg mark
```

**Flag on asafoetida:** commercial hing is almost always compounded with **wheat flour**. That makes your product (a) not gluten-free and (b) an allergen-declaration obligation, and (c) unsellable to the gluten-free wellness crowd you're targeting. Source **rice-flour-compounded or pure hing** instead. Slightly costlier, removes a real positioning problem. Same discipline applies to any anti-caking agent.

Also: **no added MSG**, and say so. "No MSG" is a factual subtraction claim, it's legal, and it neutralises the biggest objection to any savoury instant product in India.

## 6. Retail pack configurations

| Config | Contents | Purpose | Indicative MRP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trial strip | 3 sachets, one of each flavour | Café counter, gym reception, sampling | ₹99 |
| Core box | 10 sachets, single flavour | Quick commerce hero SKU | ₹249 |
| Variety box | 12 sachets (4/4/4) | Gifting, corporate, Q-comm | ₹299 |
| Institutional | 500 sachets, bulk carton, **"For institutional use only — not for retail sale"** | Hotels, banquets, corporates | No MRP required (see compliance) |

The institutional carton marking is not cosmetic — it legally exempts you from Legal Metrology retail declarations on that SKU. See `02-compliance-india.md`.
