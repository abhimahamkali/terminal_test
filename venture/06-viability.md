# 06 — Viability Assessment

**Question asked:** is this viable given ₹1 lakh of capital and a home kitchen?

**Short answer:** Yes, viable — but with a hard ceiling. The realistic outcome from a home setup is
**₹3,000 to ₹67,000/month of owner earnings by month 12**, and which end you land on depends on
almost nothing you've been planning for. It depends on landing **5–8 corporate-pantry and
banquet accounts.** Cafés and gyms cannot get you there on volume, no matter how many you sign.

The unit economics are sound. The capital is sufficient to test. The constraint is the
**volume per account**, and that's where the analysis below concentrates.

---

## 1. The three separate viability questions

| Question | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Are the unit economics viable?** | **Yes — clearly** | ₹3.15 cash COGS against a ₹9 café price is a 65% gross margin. Better than most Indian food businesses. Cost is not your problem. |
| **Is it viable at your manufacturing capability?** | **Yes, with a hard ceiling** | Solo hand-packing caps you at ~20,000 sachets/month → ~₹67,000/month owner earnings at best. Real money, but it is a job, not yet a company. |
| **Is ₹1 lakh enough capital?** | **Yes — to get a verdict** | ₹1L buys you a definitive yes/no on demand. It does *not* buy you growth, marketing, hotels or quick commerce. Treat it as the cost of the answer, not the cost of the business. |

**The thing that is genuinely uncertain is none of the above.** It is whether a savoury hot drink
has a real occasion in India at ₹90 a cup. That is testable for **₹35,000 in 8 weeks** — see §6.

---

## 2. The number that decides everything: offtake per account

This is the analysis that changes the plan. Not all accounts are remotely equal, and the
difference is roughly **20x**.

| Account type | How it sells | Realistic / month | Optimistic / month |
|---|---|---|---|
| Specialty café | Menu item at ₹90, 1–3 cups/day | **30–50** | 90 |
| Gym / yoga / pilates studio | Reception counter, 2–5/day | **50–60** | 150 |
| Co-working space (100 desks) | Pantry stock | **250** | 600 |
| **Corporate pantry (200 staff)** | Free/subsidised 4pm option | **600–700** | **1,600** |
| **Hotel in-room (100 rooms, 65% occ.)** | 1 per 3 room-nights | **650** | 1,300 |
| **Hotel banquet** | 2 events/month × ~400 pax | **800** | 2,000 |
| Retail box sold at a counter | 10 sachets per transaction | 10 per box | — |

**Read this carefully, because it inverts the intuitive plan.** One corporate pantry is worth
**15 cafés**. One hotel banquet relationship is worth **20 cafés**. If you sign 30 cafés and
nothing else, you have a business doing ~1,200 sachets/month — which loses money.

So cafés and gyms are not your volume. They are, genuinely, your **visibility, credibility and
content** — exactly the job you originally assigned to hotels. And **corporates, hotel banquets
and hotel amenity contracts are your actual revenue.**

---

## 3. Your capacity ceiling

Hand-packing, working in batches (weigh 100 → fill 100 → seal 100 → envelope 100 → label 100 —
never one at a time):

| Setup | Sachets/day | Sachets/month (25 days) |
|---|---|---|
| You, solo | 600–1,000 | **15,000–25,000** |
| You + 1 helper (₹18k/mo) | 1,600–2,000 | 40,000–50,000 |
| Your stated 6 kg/day target | 2,000 | 50,000 — **needs 2–3 people** |

Take **20,000/month solo** as your working ceiling. That caps revenue at ~₹1.8 lakh/month and
owner earnings at roughly **₹67,000/month**. Everything above that requires either wages or the
₹26L capex in `03-financials.md` §6.

**This is the honest answer to "is it viable with my manufacturing capability":** it is viable as
a ₹15–20 lakh/year owner-operated business. It is not viable as a scaled brand until you leave
the home kitchen. That's a perfectly good place to start — just don't confuse the two.

## 4. Four scenarios at month 12

All use ₹3.15 cash COGS (your own labour unpaid) and ~₹9.00 blended realisation.

### Scenario A — "Cafés and gyms only" (the failure mode)
| Accounts | Sachets/mo |
|---|---|
| 15 cafés × 30 | 450 |
| 8 gyms × 50 | 400 |
| 30 retail boxes | 300 |
| **Total** | **1,150** |

Revenue **₹10,900** · COGS ₹3,600 · GP ₹7,300 · Opex ₹20,000 → **net −₹12,700/month**

**Not a business.** This is what happens if you sign only the easy accounts. Note that it fails
*despite* 23 live accounts — which is why account count is a vanity metric here.

### Scenario B — "Base case" (breaks even)
| Accounts | Sachets/mo |
|---|---|
| 20 cafés × 40 | 800 |
| 10 gyms × 60 | 600 |
| 2 corporate pantries × 600 | 1,200 |
| 1 hotel amenity × 650 | 650 |
| 1 banquet event × 450 | 450 |
| 60 retail boxes | 600 |
| **Total** | **4,300** |

Revenue **₹38,700** · COGS ₹13,500 · GP ₹25,200 · Opex ₹22,000 → **net ≈ ₹3,200/month**

Pays for itself. Doesn't pay you. A viable side project.

### Scenario C — "Volume accounts land" (a real income)
| Accounts | Sachets/mo |
|---|---|
| 25 cafés × 50 | 1,250 |
| 12 gyms × 80 | 960 |
| 5 corporate pantries × 700 | 3,500 |
| 3 hotel amenity × 650 | 1,950 |
| 4 banquet events × 500 | 2,000 |
| 150 retail boxes | 1,500 |
| **Total** | **11,160** |

Revenue **₹1,00,400** · COGS ₹35,200 · GP ₹65,300 · Opex ₹30,000 → **net ≈ ₹35,300/month**

At 55% of solo capacity, so there's headroom. **This is the target.**

### Scenario D — "Near capacity" (the decision point)
18,000 sachets/month.

Revenue **₹1,62,000** · COGS ₹56,700 · GP ₹1,05,300 · Opex ₹38,000 *(1 full-time helper)* → **net ≈ ₹67,300/month**

At ~90% of solo capacity. Here you must either stop growing or commit the ₹26L for a real line.

**The gap between A and C is five corporate accounts and four banquet events.** Not fifty cafés.

## 5. The uncomfortable finding: compliance is on the critical path after all

You wanted to test viability first and fix compliance later. That's right for the *demand test* —
and wrong for the *viability verdict*, for a specific reason:

- Scenario A (cafés, gyms) needs almost no paperwork. **And Scenario A loses money.**
- Scenarios C and D depend on corporate pantries, hotel amenity contracts and banquets. **Every
  one of those buyers runs vendor onboarding** — GST, FSSAI with the correct premises and product
  category, often a State Licence.

So the channels that make this viable are gated on exactly the paperwork you wanted to defer.

**The resolution is timing, not priority.** Weeks 1–8: test demand in cafés and gyms with minimal
paperwork (~₹35,000). Weeks 3–8, *in parallel and cheaply*: fix FSSAI status/category, register
GST and Udyam (~₹13,000 total, mostly your CA's time, not yours). By week 9 you have both a demand
signal and the documents to chase the accounts that actually pay. Don't run these in sequence —
you'd waste a quarter.

## 6. The ₹35,000 / 8-week minimum viable test

Don't spend ₹1 lakh to find out. Spend ₹35,000.

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Ingredients: formulation trials + first 10 kg of blend | 8,000 |
| 1,000 filter bags + 1,000 envelopes + 500 stick packs | 2,700 |
| Impulse sealer, 0.01 g scale, sieves, containers, gloves | 7,500 |
| Plain digital labels, 1,000 | 1,200 |
| Sampling kit: clear glasses, flask, carry case | 2,500 |
| Travel for 20 tastings (Hyderabad) | 3,000 |
| Production materials, 3,000 sachets | 5,000 |
| Buffer | 5,100 |
| **Total** | **₹35,000** |

**What it buys:** a validated format (blind test vs home-made rasam), 20 in-person tastings,
5 café menu listings, and **8 weeks of actual offtake data.**

**The one metric that decides the business:**

> **Average sachets per listed café per month, and whether they reorder.**

| Result after 8 weeks | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **< 20/café/month, < half reorder** | **Kill it.** The occasion isn't real at ₹90. You've spent ₹35,000 and learned something worth far more. |
| **20–40/café/month, some reorder** | **Pause and reframe.** The product works, the positioning doesn't. Revisit `00-strategy.md` before more spend. |
| **≥ 40/café/month, ≥ 3 of 5 reorder** | **Go.** Deploy the remaining ₹65,000 and pivot your selling effort to corporate pantries and banquets immediately. |

Reorder rate matters more than volume. A café that sells 60 and doesn't reorder tried a novelty.
A café that sells 30 and reorders three months running has a habit forming.

## 7. What would have to be true

| # | Assumption | Confidence | How you test it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Indians will drink a hot savoury drink outside a meal | **Low–moderate** | The 8-week café test (§6) |
| 2 | A café customer pays ₹90 for it | Moderate | Same test |
| 3 | A café keeps it on the menu (reorders) | **The key unknown** | Same test |
| 4 | You can land 5+ corporate/banquet accounts | Moderate–high | Month 3–6; gated on GST + FSSAI |
| 5 | You can produce 15–20k/month from home at consistent quality | **High** | Time yourself packing 100 in week 2 |
| 6 | 3 g in a bag delivers recognisable rasam | **Uncertain — biggest technical risk** | Week-1 blind test (`01-product-packaging.md` §2) |

Assumptions 1, 3 and 6 are the business. Two of the three are answerable inside ₹35,000.

## 8. Honest market read

**Against you:**
- Rasam instants and rasam cubes already exist and have not built a market. That is real negative evidence, and you spotted it yourself.
- The closest *proven* Indian savoury-drink categories sit at **low price points**: packaged buttermilk (Amul Masti, Mother Dairy) works at ₹10–15, and instant soup (Knorr) is a modest category at ₹15–25 a serve. Nothing proves Indians will pay ₹90 for a savoury cup.
- You are creating an occasion, not filling one. That is the most expensive kind of marketing, and you have no marketing budget.

**For you:**
- The prior failures all failed **in the rasam frame at commodity price points.** None tried the format-plus-framing route (teabag ritual, café menu item, zero-sugar/caffeine-free). That doesn't guarantee it works, but it means the prior failures aren't a clean read on your specific bet.
- Packaged buttermilk proves Indians **do** buy savoury drinks. The question is price and temperature, not the flavour category.
- **Most importantly: your B2B-first instinct materially de-risks this.** In B2B you are not selling a new consumer category — you are selling operational facts. A café buys a ₹90 menu item with ₹9 cost and zero prep. A corporate buys a caffeine-free pantry option. A hotel buys a branded amenity with no wastage. Those are concrete, verifiable value propositions. **The consumer only has to not dislike it.** The retail/Blinkit path, by contrast, needs consumers to actively want a new category — which is why it correctly sits at month 7+.

**Net:** moderate confidence on the B2B path, low confidence on the mass-retail path. You picked the right one.

---

## 9. Verdict

**Proceed — but with the ₹35,000 test, not the ₹1 lakh plan.**

1. **Spend ₹35,000 over 8 weeks** to answer the only question that matters: does a café reorder?
2. **In parallel, cheaply:** fix FSSAI (status, premises, category), register GST and Udyam (~₹13,000). These unlock the channels that make it viable, so don't sequence them after.
3. **At week 8, decide** on the thresholds in §6. Kill, reframe, or go.
4. **If go:** deploy the remaining ₹52,000 and redirect your selling effort from cafés to **corporate pantries and hotel banquets.** That's where Scenario C lives.
5. **Plan for the ceiling.** ~₹67,000/month owner earnings is the roof from a home kitchen. Decide *before* you hit it whether you want to raise and break through, or run it as a profitable owner-operated business. Both are legitimate; drifting into the ceiling unprepared is not.

**Biggest risk to manage:** signing thirty cafés, feeling successful, and discovering at month 12
that you've built Scenario A. Track **sachets per account**, not account count.
