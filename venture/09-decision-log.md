## A. Decisions taken

### Positioning & brand

| # | Decision | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-01 | **Don't sell rasam. Create the "savoury hot drink" category.** | **Locked** | At ~₹3.50 COGS for 3 g, the ₹1–3/g price point the founder referenced is below cost. The product is unviable as a rasam commodity and makes 60%+ GM as a hot drink at ₹15–25. **The pricing is the positioning.** |
| D-02 | **"Rasam" moves to small type** — the ingredient/heritage line on the back, never the headline. | **Locked** | Heritage there adds credibility; heritage in the headline caps price. Precedent: bone broth — same liquid, repriced entirely by reframing. |
| D-03 | **Price-setting noun: "brew"** (brand) and **"shot"** (café menu). | **Locked** | The noun silently sets the price ceiling. Brew/shot borrow coffee's price permission (₹100–250). |
| D-04 | **Tagline: "Savoury, not sweet."** | Recommended | Mirrors zero-sugar sentence structure, no regulated claim, three words. Chosen over "Salt, not sugar" because of Indian sodium/hypertension anxiety. |
| D-05 | **Insight line: "Every drink in India is sweet… here's the first that isn't."** | **Locked — best asset** | True, verifiable in three seconds, makes an invisible gap visible. Requires no explanation of rasam and no health claim. |
| D-06 | **Occasion: 4 PM primary, after-dinner secondary.** | **Locked** | The one moment both chai and coffee genuinely fail. The caffeine/sleep conversation is already running in the target audience. |
| D-07 | **Stat block: `0g SUGAR · 0mg CAFFEINE · 0 DAIRY · ~6 KCAL`** | **Locked** | All four are permitted nutrient-content claims — free, legal, no substantiation dossier. The entire legal marketing arsenal. |
| D-08 | **SKU names: Pepper / Lemon / Beet.** | **Locked** | Reads as varietals (coffee roast) not flavours (soft drink). No "rasam". |
| D-09 | **Brand name: TAMRA** (Sanskrit, copper). | **Provisional — pending screening** | Arbitrary mark → strongest Class 30 filing. Indian-rooted, not a food word, accidental phonetic nod to tamarind, hands over a copper visual identity. Alternatives: SAVRA, LAVAN. |
| D-10 | **Gut health lives in culture, never in print.** | **Locked** | A printed health claim triggers FSSAI's Health Supplements & Nutraceuticals regime and destroys the founder's own "easy to register" requirement. Ingredient-led implication is legal; physiological promises are not. |

### Product & operations

| # | Decision | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-11 | **Both formats from launch**: pyramid/filter infusion bag + stick pack. | **Locked (founder)** | Infusion bag = hotels/cafés/retail (theatre, no residue). Stick pack = gyms/corporates/bulk (cheaper, better taste fidelity). |
| D-12 | **Metallised laminate envelope, not paper/kraft/glassine.** | **Locked** | Tamarind + salt + fines are strongly hygroscopic. Paper fails within weeks — and it fails *after* a customer has stocked 2,000 units. |
| D-13 | **Rice-flour-compounded (or pure) hing, never wheat-compounded.** | **Locked** | Commercial hing is usually cut with wheat flour → allergen declaration obligation *and* unsellable to the gluten-free wellness audience being targeted. |
| D-14 | **"No added MSG" on pack.** | **Locked** | Factual subtraction claim; neutralises the biggest objection to any Indian instant savoury product. |
| D-15 | **Two-layer packaging: one generic printed envelope + digitally printed labels** for hotel branding. | **Locked** | Solves the founder's "same costing, two varieties" constraint. Avoids the rotogravure trap (₹25–40k per cylinder, ~50,000-envelope MOQ *per design*). |
| D-16 | **Institutional cartons marked "For institutional use only — not for retail sale."** | **Locked** | Legal Metrology exemption: no MRP required, fewer declarations, and hotel pricing stays confidential so it can't undercut the ₹249 retail box. |
| D-17 | **Co-packer, not own line.** | **Locked** | Avoids ₹2.5L tooling before the format is proven, and a co-packer's licensed premises is the *only* route to hotel vendor onboarding from a home setup. |

## B. Options rejected — and why

**Read this before reopening any of them.**

| # | Rejected | Why |
|---|---|---|
| R-01 | **"Rasam" or "Instant" in large type** | Both are price ceilings. "Instant" is a discount signal (Maggi-adjacent). Founder's own intuition, confirmed by the COGS arithmetic. |
| R-02 | **"Tea" as the category noun** | **The trap.** It's comprehensible *and* format-accurate (it literally is a tea bag) — which is exactly why it's tempting. It anchors at ₹20–60/cup, a third of what's needed. |
| R-03 | **"Soup" as the noun** | Anchors at a meal, not at 4pm. Puts you at lunch. |
| R-04 | **Brand name `NOON`** | Three failures: (1) occasion mismatch — the occasion is 4pm, so the name argues against the hook; (2) **noon.com** is a major Gulf trademark holder, closing the first export region an Indian food brand targets; (3) **noon chai** is already a Kashmiri *salted savoury tea* — *noon* means salt in Kashmiri. Naming a savoury brew after an existing savoury brew. |
| R-05 | **Brand name `4PM`** | (1) Numerals/descriptive marks attract absolute-grounds objections under **Section 9** for lacking distinctiveness — a weak filing; (2) welds the brand to one occasion when gyms (morning/evening) and after-dinner are both in scope. |
| R-06 | **Brand names `CHARU` / `SAARU`** | *Charu* is the **Telugu word for rasam**. In Hyderabad and Andhra — the entire launch market — it reinstates the exact commodity anchor the repositioning exists to escape. A heritage word only works where the audience doesn't already use it as the grocery word. |
| R-07 | **The chug challenge** ("how many shots in 20 seconds") | (1) **Safety** — speed-drinking *hot* liquid risks mouth/throat/oesophageal scalding; a brand that organises it owns that outcome, can't run it with minors, can't defend an on-camera injury. (2) **It frames the product as an ordeal**, telling viewers it tastes bad — the exact opposite of "savoury, not sweet". The mechanic wanted is aspiration (*I'm the kind of person who drinks this*), not endurance (*I survived this*). (3) A dare buys views, not the reorders viability rests on. **Redirected to D-34 and D-36, which keep the group, timer, glass and leaderboard.** |
| R-08 | **Hotels as the first channel** | Home-premises producer fails hotel vendor onboarding (licensed premises, often State Licence + ISO 22000, sometimes a physical audit). Not fixable by relationship. Deferred to month 4+ via a co-packer's premises. |
| R-09 | **A D2C store** | Founder's own call, and correct: CAC, logistics, returns and support for a ₹249 order. The website's only job is a credible B2B catalogue + lead form. |
| R-10 | **Quick commerce at launch** | Listing a product nobody searches for means paying for every impression; a no-velocity listing gets algorithmically buried and is harder to revive than to launch. Month 7–9, 2–3 pincodes, one city. |
| R-11 | **A 4th flavour** | Each SKU is another ~₹40k of lab testing, another artwork, another MOQ, another slow-mover. Three is already one more than needed. |
| R-12 | **Large-name endorsement (e.g. Rana) now** | Expensive, unreliable, and generates a demand spike unservicable at 2,000 sachets/day. Revisit in Phase 2. |
| R-13 | **Own manufacturing line now** | ₹10–15L for an automatic teabag machine before the format is even validated. Co-pack first. |
| R-14 | **Printed gut-health / digestive / debloat / appetising claims** | Regulated health claims under FSS (Advertising and Claims) Regulations 2018; risks reclassification into the nutraceutical regime. Hard stop. |
| R-15 | **"Natural", "pure", "fresh"** | Separately restricted terms under the labelling regulations. |
| R-16 | **"Grandmother's traditional recipe" framing** | True, but it ages the brand straight out of the Gen-Z audience the founder specifically wants. Let the ingredient list imply heritage; keep the voice modern. |
| R-17 | **Modern trade / supermarket listings in year 1** | Listing fees, slotting, 90-day terms, and no velocity — paying for shelf space nobody walks to. |
| R-18 | **Export in year 1** | Needs FSSAI **Central** Licence, IEC, APEDA, destination labelling and a validated shelf life. Phase 3, as the founder planned. |
| R-19 | **Kraft/paper "eco premium" envelope** | Will fail on moisture — and fail after a customer has stocked it. See D-12. |
