# 04 — Go-to-Market & Execution Plan

## 1. The hotel B2B playbook

### Know who actually decides

You are not selling to "a hotel". You are selling to one of four different buyers, and the pitch is different for each:

| Entry point | Decision maker | What they care about | Your pitch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **In-room amenity tray** | Housekeeping Manager → Purchase Manager → GM | Cost per occupied room, zero wastage, guest delight, no training | "Costs less than your tea bag, needs no prep, and guests photograph it" |
| **Welcome drink** | F&B Manager → Executive Chef → GM | Labour, wastage, consistency, differentiation | "Your juice welcome takes a person and spoils. This takes 20 seconds and zero wastage" |
| **Banquets / conference breaks** | Banquet Sales + Exec Chef | Per-head cost, the caffeine-free ask that keeps coming up | "The option for the 30% of your delegates who don't want a 4pm coffee" |
| **Spa / gym / wellness floor** | Spa Manager | On-brand wellness credibility | "Zero sugar, zero caffeine, zero dairy, plant-based" |

**Lead with the in-room amenity tray.** It's the lowest-risk, lowest-cost yes, it requires no chef to change a recipe, and it puts your product in a guest's hand in a premium context — which is the entire point of the hotel channel. Then expand inside the account to banquets, which is where the volume is.

### The pitch, in one line

> *"The caffeine-free, zero-sugar hot drink for guests who don't want tea or coffee — served in 20 seconds, zero prep, zero wastage, and it carries your logo."*

Note what that sentence does not contain: the words rasam, instant, or masala. Note what it does contain: the hotel's own logo. Custom branding is not a cost you absorb — it is the *reason they say yes*, because it's an amenity that looks bespoke to their property at a commodity price.

### The sampling protocol (this is the whole sale)

Nobody buys a novel hot drink from a deck. The sale happens in the tasting.

1. **Never pitch before they taste.** Ask for 10 minutes with the Exec Chef and bring a kettle, three clear glasses, and the three flavours.
2. **Serve it in the right vessel.** A clear glass espresso cup, not a paper cup. The visual is half the product — especially beetroot.
3. **Let the format do the talking.** Hand them the bag and let *them* dip it. The theatre is the differentiator.
4. **Leave a 100-sachet trial pack free**, with their logo already on the labels (digital labels, ~₹0.65 each — a ₹65 investment per account). A mocked-up custom label is the highest-ROI sales asset you have.
5. **Ask for a 30-day in-room trial in 20 rooms**, not a purchase order. Small yes first.
6. **Come back with the data.** Consumption rate per room-night, guest feedback if housekeeping collected any. Then ask for the full property.

### Minerva, Avira and the regional-chain advantage

Regional chains are the right first target and better than the big international brands, for a specific reason: **the GM can actually decide.** At a Marriott or Taj property, an amenity change routes through a corporate procurement framework and a global supplier list — a 6-month-minimum process that a 6 kg/day producer cannot survive. A regional chain's GM plus Exec Chef can approve a 20-room trial in one meeting.

Sequence: **one property → prove consumption → the chain's F&B head → all properties.** A 6-property chain at 100 rooms each is ~4,000 sachets/month. Four such chains fills most of your Phase 1 capacity.

### What their purchase desk will ask you for — have it ready before the first meeting

Being unprepared here loses deals you have already won on taste:
- FSSAI licence copy (**State Licence, not Basic** — see `02-compliance-india.md` §2)
- GST registration certificate
- Product specification sheet + nutritional panel
- NABL lab test reports (micro, heavy metals, pesticide residue)
- Shelf-life substantiation
- Allergen declaration
- Sample batch COA
- Company registration / Udyam certificate
- Cancelled cheque + vendor registration form
- Liability insurance (some chains ask — ₹15–25k/year, get it)
- Increasingly: **ISO 22000 or HACCP** (Phase 2, but ask early so you know which accounts need it)

Assemble this as a single PDF vendor pack. It converts a two-week back-and-forth into a same-day onboarding.

## 2. Using Deepthi Nallamothu and Saketh correctly

Your instinct is right: **use them for access, not endorsement.** An endorsement from a regional name buys you a spike of attention you cannot service at 6 kg/day. An introduction buys you a meeting that would otherwise take three months of cold calling.

**The ask, specifically:**

> *"Can you WhatsApp-introduce me to the GM at [named property]? Two lines is all I need — that I'm a friend and worth 15 minutes. I'll take it from there."*

That is the entire ask. Name the specific person. Don't ask them to "help with hotels" — vague asks get vague results and burn the favour.

**Rules for not wasting the relationship:**
- **One named introduction per ask.** Never a list.
- **Report back within a week**, whether it worked or not. This is what makes a second ask possible.
- **Offer reciprocity up front** — a case for their own use, a custom-branded run for an event they're hosting, first look at the brand. Make the exchange explicit rather than leaving them feeling used.
- **Do not put their name in your deck** or on your Instagram without written permission. Once you do, the relationship becomes a transaction and the access dries up.
- **Keep them out of the marketing entirely for now.** Revisit endorsement only when you're past 20 kg/day and can absorb demand.

## 3. Café and gym channel

Structurally your best channel: fast payment, high margin, and they market the product *for* you by putting it on a menu.

**Cafés — target plant-based, specialty-coffee and wellness cafés first.**
- The pitch is a **menu item**, not an ingredient: *"Savoury Shot — ₹90"*. You sell at ₹9, they sell at ₹90, zero prep, zero wastage, zero training. There is no other item on their menu with those economics.
- Their incentive to push it is enormous. Let them keep all of it.
- **Ask for the menu listing as a condition of the trade price.** A listed menu item is a permanent billboard; a sachet in their store cupboard is nothing.
- Supply a small branded counter display with trial strips (`01-product-packaging.md` §6) so a café visit converts to a take-home purchase.

**Gyms and studios — lead with beetroot and lemon.**
- Frame it as **savoury electrolytes**, the LMNT analogue (`00-strategy.md` §2). Salty, warm, zero sugar, post-workout. This is a genuinely differentiated proposition in Indian gyms, where the only options are sugary shakes and water.
- Reception-counter sales at ₹40–50 per cup is a real revenue line for a gym owner with no equipment needed.
- Yoga, pilates and CrossFit studios over commercial chains — smaller decision loop, more aligned clientele.

## 4. Quick commerce — enter late, deliberately

Do **not** list on Blinkit/Zepto/Instamart in month 1. Reasons:
- Listing a product nobody is searching for means paying for every impression via ads. You will burn ₹2–3 lakh discovering that nobody searches for a savoury hot drink.
- A new listing with no velocity gets deprioritised by the platform's algorithm, and recovering a dead listing is harder than launching fresh.

**Enter when you can demonstrate search demand** — when hotel and café sampling has produced people typing your brand name into the app. Target month 7–9. Then:
- Launch with **one hero SKU** (variety box of 12) in **2–3 pincodes** in your home city, not nationally.
- Budget ads as a real line (15–25% of GMV for a new brand) and treat the first 90 days as paid learning.
- Optimise the listing image for the *stat block*, not the product — `0 SUGAR · 0 CAFFEINE · 6 KCAL` should be legible at thumbnail size. Nobody can see a sachet at 200px.

## 5. Content and influencer plan

**The content job in year one is category education, not product selling.** People don't know savoury hot drinks exist. Fix that, then own the category you created.

**Three content pillars:**
1. **The category** — miso, bone broth, bouillon, consommé, rasam, sotanghon. "The world drinks savoury. India doesn't. Yet." This makes you the authority rather than a vendor.
2. **The ritual** — the savoury shot. The clear glass, the steam, the string over the rim, the beetroot colour. One product-centric frame daily, as you planned. The format is the content.
3. **The subtraction stat block** — 0 sugar, 0 caffeine, 0 dairy, 6 kcal. Never a health claim (`02-compliance-india.md` §2). Always a number.

**Influencer approach:**
- **Micro over macro.** 20 creators at 20–80k followers in food/fitness/wellness beats one 2M-follower post, costs less, and — critically — generates demand you can actually service at 6 kg/day.
- **Gym and yoga creators over food creators.** Food creators will frame it as "instant rasam", which undoes your entire positioning. Fitness creators will frame it as a zero-sugar savoury drink. Brief them accordingly.
- **Written brief on every collaboration**, restricting them to sensory and subtraction language. A paid creator's health claim is legally your claim (`02-compliance-india.md` §2).
- **The café menu is worth more than the creators.** If you must choose, spend the money getting onto 10 specialty café menus.

**On Rana / large-name endorsement:** park it. Expensive, unreliable, and it generates a demand spike you cannot fill at 2,000 sachets/day. Revisit in Phase 2.

## 6. 90-day execution plan

### Days 1–30 · Prove the product

| # | Task | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Format bake-off.** Flat filter vs pyramid mesh vs stick pack, blind-tested against home-made rasam with 10 people | The format decision. Everything else waits on this. |
| 2 | Lock 3 formulations; source **rice-flour-compounded hing** (gluten flag, `01-product-packaging.md` §5) | Final recipes |
| 3 | Shortlist 3 co-packers with a live FSSAI licence and teabag capability | Co-packer LOI |
| 4 | Trademark clearance search on 4-name shortlist, Classes 30 + 32 | Cleared name |
| 5 | File Pvt Ltd (SPICe+), GST, Udyam | Entity live |
| 6 | Apply FSSAI (State Licence, manufacturer category) | Application filed |
| 7 | **CA confirms HSN + GST rate in writing** (see `02-compliance-india.md` §5) | Written opinion |

### Days 31–60 · Prove the pitch

| # | Task | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | Brand identity + label artwork; generic envelope + digital hotel-label template | Artwork files |
| 9 | Send 1 SKU for nutritional + micro panel at a NABL lab | Test reports |
| 10 | Hand-pack 3,000 sachets; build 40 sample kits with mocked custom labels | Sampling inventory |
| 11 | Assemble the **vendor pack PDF** (§1) | Sales asset |
| 12 | Ask Deepthi and Saketh for **2 named introductions each** (§2) | 4 warm meetings |
| 13 | Pitch 15 accounts: 5 hotels, 6 cafés, 4 gyms | 15 tastings |
| 14 | Convert 3 hotel **20-room trials** (not POs) | 3 live trials |

### Days 61–90 · Prove it repeats

| # | Task | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | Collect consumption data from the room trials; convert 2 to full-property POs | First real revenue |
| 16 | Land **5 café menu listings** with "Savoury Shot" on the printed menu | Permanent visibility |
| 17 | Land 2 gyms + **1 corporate pantry** (the easy sale you hadn't listed) | Diversified, fast-paying revenue |
| 18 | Launch Instagram: 30 posts across the 3 pillars (§5) | Content engine running |
| 19 | Brief and run 5 micro-creators | First organic demand |
| 20 | Complete remaining SKU lab panels + accelerated shelf-life study | Retail-ready compliance |
| 21 | **Decide Phase 2:** does the demand justify 20 kg/day capex? | Go/no-go on ₹26L |

**Day-90 success criteria — be strict with yourself:**
- Product beats home-made rasam in blind test, or you have reformulated until it does
- **10 paying accounts**, of which ≥3 have placed a **repeat** order
- ≥5 café menus carrying the product
- Blended realisation ≥ **₹8.00/sachet** (i.e. you did not buy your way in on price)
- Monthly run rate ≥ 15,000 sachets
- Vendor pack complete, so onboarding is same-day

If you miss the repeat-order criterion, the problem is the product or the occasion — not the sales effort. Go back to §1 of `00-strategy.md` before spending more.

## 7. What to deliberately NOT do in year one

- **No D2C store.** You said B2B only and you're right. A D2C site means CAC, logistics, returns and customer service for a ₹249 order. The website's only job is to be a credible B2B catalogue and lead form.
- **No national quick-commerce launch.** 2–3 pincodes, one city (§4).
- **No modern trade / supermarket listings.** Listing fees, slotting, 90-day terms and no velocity. You'd be paying for shelf space nobody walks to.
- **No export.** Requires FSSAI Central Licence, IEC, APEDA, destination-country labelling and a shelf-life you haven't validated. Phase 3, as you planned.
- **No fourth flavour.** Every SKU is another ₹40k of lab testing, another artwork, another MOQ and another slow-mover. Three is already one more than you need.
- **No large-name endorsement.** You cannot service the spike.
- **No own manufacturing line** until the format is proven and a co-packer is straining. Co-pack first.
